THE PROBLEM

The agent did the thinking.
A person did the paying.

An agent works out that the warehouse stocks out in three days. It drafts the purchase order. Then it sends someone a notification — and a human opens a supplier portal and types in a card number.

Every “AI for operations” product stops at exactly this wall.

WHY NOBODY FIXED IT

Giving the agent a card
is the worse option.

Now a model with a plausible hallucination has your card.

Finance teams haven’t handed spend authority to agents because agents are bad at deciding what to buy. They haven’t because nobody could answer one question:

Who authorized this, and what stopped it going further?

THE INSIGHT

Two limits. One decision.

THE AGENT’S THRESHOLD

What we let an agent do without a human. A line in our own config — which a bug can step over.

THE MANDATE CAP

What the card network will actually authorize. Enforced by Visa — but blind to whether this spend is sensible.

A spend is automatic only when both agree. Everything else escalates, or is refused.

THE CARD

Three questions, in the order
an approver actually asks them.

01
Why is this my decision?
Over the agent’s limit. No standing authorization. Budget exhausted. This leads the card — everything else is context.
02
What authorized it?
The policy clause, quoted, with its source. Not the agent’s own reasoning.
03
Where does the money come from?
The mandate and what’s left on it — in words, never an id.
